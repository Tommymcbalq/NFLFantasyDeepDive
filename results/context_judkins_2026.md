# Quinshon Judkins (RB, CLE) — 2026 Context Research

Research date: **2026-08-09**. ADP 49.8 (RB22). Board value 11.36 PPG (75% shrunk to price).

Labels used throughout:
- **[F]** verified fact — transaction, official stat, on-record quote
- **[R]** reported / beat-writer speculation — someone's opinion or projection
- **[I]** my inference — not sourced, derived by me

**Verdict up front:** the owner's *situation* premise is largely correct and well-documented.
The *inference* he draws from it — that improved inputs will convert to a large PPG gain — is
much weaker than he thinks, and the reason is not efficiency. It is that Judkins is a
**50.3%-snap two-down back**, not a bell cow, on a team Vegas prices at 5.5 wins.

## 1. The efficiency artifacts

### 1a. The "more yards after contact than total yards" claim — **FALSE, but he is remembering a real stat**

**[F]** The stat he is remembering is almost certainly SI/Browns Digest, **2025-11-18**: through
that point Judkins had **555 yards after contact of 620 total rushing yards — 89.5%**, 10th in the
NFL in YAC on 17th-most rushing yards.
([SI](https://www.si.com/nfl/browns/onsi/news/this-crazy-stat-shows-how-good-quinshon-judkins-has-been-for-browns-01ka9w8cx63v), 2025-11-18)

**[F]** Full 2025 season, PFF: **230 att, 827 yds, 3.6 YPC, 3.2 YAC/att (16th among HBs)**.
3.2 × 230 = 736 YAC of 827 total = **89.0%**.
([PFF player page](https://www.pff.com/nfl/players/quinshon-judkins/157128), retrieved 2026-08-09)

So: ~89%, **not** above 100%. The claim as stated is wrong. It is, however, an extraordinary
share — and it is *not* primarily a compliment. See 1b.

### 1b. Two different, both-internally-consistent decompositions — do not mix them

There are two public YBC/YAC splits in circulation with **different definitions of the contact
point**, and they must not be averaged or cross-quoted:

| Source | YBC/att | YAC/att | Sum | League context |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PFF-lineage (FantasyLife, 2026-07-21) | **0.40** | **3.2** | 3.60 | YBC **worst among qualified rushers**; YAC 16th |
| Sharp-lineage (Yahoo/Dawg Pound Daily, 2026-07-02) | **1.47** | **2.12** | 3.59 | YBC **51st of 65** (50+ carries); YAC **47th of 65** |

**[F]** Both sums equal his 3.6 YPC exactly, so each pair is coherent on its own terms.
Sources: [FantasyLife 2026-07-21](https://www.fantasylife.com/articles/fantasy/quinshon-judkins-fantasy-football-outlook-the-perfect-mid-round);
[Yahoo Sports 2026-07-02](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/one-overlooked-stat-changes-quinshon-150006892.html).

**[I]** This matters a lot and cuts against the owner. Under the **PFF** definition Judkins looks
like an elite contact-breaker (3.2 YAC/att, 16th). Under the **Sharp** definition he is
**below average after contact too** (2.12, 47th of 65). The two agree completely on the blocking
(bottom-decile YBC) and disagree completely on the runner. The owner is implicitly quoting the
PFF number for the runner and treating the bad-blocking number as the whole story. **The
after-contact evidence for Judkins being good is definition-dependent and therefore weak.**

**[F]** A third source (4for4 / FantasyPros, 49 qualifiers) puts him **26th in YAC/att, 33rd in
missed-tackle rate, and 40th in explosive-run rate** — i.e. middle-to-below-average after contact,
siding with the pessimistic decomposition. He was 4th-fewest in yards *before* contact.

**[F]** Corroborating the pessimistic read — his college profile was *also* unimpressive after
contact: **3.04 YAC/att at Ohio State in 2024, 121st of 161 FBS RBs with 100+ carries**
([On Tap Sports Net / SI draft profiles](https://ontapsportsnet.com/nfl/2025-nfl-draft-scouting-report-quinshon-judkins/), Mar 2025).
That is a second, independent, *situation-free* observation that he is not a special
contact-breaker. This is the single most damaging fact I found for the thesis.

### 1c. Stuffed rate and box counts — **owner is correct, strongly**

**[F]** **Stuffed rate 22.2%, 8th-highest in the league** (FantasyLife, 2026-07-21).
**[F]** **Hit-at-or-behind-the-line rate 49.1%, 6th-worst**; Browns team YBC **0.65, dead last**
([4for4, 2026-07-21](https://www.4for4.com/2026/preseason/quinshon-judkins-has-fantasy-football-workload-needs-browns-help)).
**[F]** **8+ men in box on 45.22% of his runs — highest in the NFL**, per Next Gen Stats.
Next-highest RB was Derrick Henry at 39.8%; Ashton Jeanty saw 9.1%. **7.7 defenders in box per
rush, 3rd-highest** among RBs with 100+ carries.
**[F]** Only **25% of carries gained 5+ yards — dead last of 55 RBs with 75+ carries** (Yahoo, 2026-07-02).

**[I]** League-average 8+ box rate for RBs is roughly 20–25%; I could not source a clean 2025
league-average figure and searched for it specifically — treat 45.22% as "extreme outlier,
roughly double typical" rather than a precise multiple.

### 1d. The split nobody is mentioning — **strong evidence against a pure-situation story**

**[F]** Through 2025-10-09 (5 games), Judkins had **72 carries, 347 yards, 4.8 YPC**, with
**+0.05 EPA/rush (5th of 34 qualifying backs)** and **+5 first downs over expected (2nd in NFL)**
([ESPN, Barnwell, 2025-10-09](https://www.espn.com/espn/print?id=46519192)).
**[F]** Season final: 230 carries, 827 yards, 3.6 YPC.

**[I]** Arithmetic: his final **158 carries produced 480 yards — 3.04 YPC**. The offensive line,
the QBs and the box counts were bad in both halves. A situation-only explanation does not
account for a 1.8-YPC in-season collapse. Either defenses adjusted to him specifically, or the
hot 72-carry start was noise. Both readings argue the 4.8 was the outlier, not the 3.6.

### 1e. Summary of the efficiency question

The **environment** claims are verified and are genuinely league-worst. The **"the runner is
better than the numbers"** claim is not established: one metric family says yes, an equally
credible one says no, and his college tape metric agrees with the pessimistic one.

**[F] Sampson 2025:** 175 rush yards; **33 rec on 40 targets, 271 yds, 2 TD** — he **out-targeted

**[I] Bell-cow verdict: NO — he is a high-volume two-down early-down back.**
The 66.1% rush share (3rd in NFL) is what makes him look like a bell cow and is what the owner is

profile. In PPR this is the binding constraint on his ceiling: he has no receiving floor, so his
weekly outcome is entirely a function of rushing efficiency and TDs, both of which are volatile.
His 2025 PPG of 11.1 came *with* a 3rd-best-in-NFL carry share — there is very little volume
headroom left. The upside has to come from efficiency and receiving work, and the receiving work
is Sampson's.

**[F] The strongest on-record counter to my verdict** — RB coach **Duce Staley, 2026-07-31**: Judkins
"worked his ass off," "the sky is the limit," and the Browns think he **"can play all three downs."**

**[I]** This is a coach in July saying a positive thing about his starter, which is close to
uninformative on its own — but it is the only direct evidence that the third-down role is
contestable, and it should be weighted as a real (if weak) upside branch. Note also **[R]** that
Monken's own head-coaching history is feature-back-leaning rather than committee (Ok. State: Randle
208+ carries; Tampa 2018: Peyton Barber 234 carries with Ronald Jones held to 23).

**[R]** The rest of the bull framing, stated fairly: Monken's Ravens finished **1st and
2nd in total rush attempts** in his last two seasons; he ranks **1st among 2026 projected
playcallers in 10+ yard designed run rate (14.3% over 63 career games)**; his staple is **outside

**[I]** Caveat: Monken's Baltimore run tilt is confounded by Lamar Jackson and Derrick Henry, and
he was notably more pass-friendly in Tampa and in his earlier Cleveland stop. Treat "Monken is
run-heavy" as a personnel-driven, not fixed, identity. Also note that a run-heavy scheme raises
*carries*, and Judkins already has near-max carry share; it does little for his PPR receiving gap.

## 7. Bottom line for the model

**Where the owner is right [F]:** the environment was verifiably league-worst — highest 8+ box rate
in the NFL (45.22%), dead-last team yards before contact (0.65), 8th-worst stuffed rate (22.2%),
dead-last PFF pass block grade, worst-in-NFL EPA/dropback (-0.270), last in points per drive. The
line was genuinely rebuilt with real capital, and the passing game genuinely cannot get worse.

**Where he is wrong or unsupported:**
- The "more YAC than total yards" stat is **false** (it is ~89%, from a Nov 2025 midseason snapshot).
- After-contact excellence is **definition-dependent** — 3.2/att (16th) under PFF, 2.12/att (47th of 65)
  under Sharp — and his college number (121st of 161) sides with the pessimistic version.
- Jeudy **is** the stated WR1 on the record from the GM; only the target share is contested.
- Bell-cow status is **not established**: 50.3% snap rate, 32nd among RBs.

**Strongest single argument against the thesis [I]:** even granting every environmental improvement
in full, Judkins' 2025 11.1 PPG was produced on the **3rd-largest rush share in the NFL**. There is
no volume left to add, the receiving role belongs to Sampson, and the Garrett trade pushes game
script the wrong way. The thesis requires the efficiency gain to arrive *alone*, and the two
situation-free reads on him — his college after-contact percentile and his 3.04 YPC over the final
158 carries of 2025 — both say he is closer to an average runner than a suppressed good one.

**[I] Model implication:** the 75% shrink toward ADP is defensible here. The context research does
not justify moving him meaningfully above 11.36 PPG, and the archetype/role covariate should encode
him as **two-down early-down runner, not bell cow** — which is the material correction to the
owner's prior.
